## SECTION 1: THE PSYCHOLOGY OF THE FIRST 2 SECONDS

### Why 2 Seconds Matters (Not 3, Not 5)

TikTok measures drop-off at the 3-second mark. Here's what happens:

1. **Milliseconds 0-300:** Your brain decides if this is worth watching
2. **Milliseconds 300-1000:** You're reading the on-screen text
3. **Seconds 1-2:** You're deciding whether to scroll or stay
4. **Second 3:** TikTok's algorithm counts: "Did they stay past 3 seconds?"

If 50% of viewers scroll by second 3, TikTok KILLS the video in the algorithm. It doesn't promote it. It doesn't show it to more people. It gets buried.

Your job: Make the first 2 seconds so gripping that even people actively trying to scroll can't stop watching.

### The 3 Jobs of the First 2 Seconds

Every successful hook does ALL THREE:

1. **STOPS THE THUMB** — Breaks the scroll pattern
   - How: Movement + contrast + something unexpected
   - Not how: Static shots, slow motion, talking heads without movement
   - Example: Tyler claps hands hard (stops thumb) vs. Tyler sits still (doesn't work)

2. **CREATES CURIOSITY** — Makes them NEED to watch to close a gap
   - How: Ask a question without answering it
   - Not how: Give them the answer immediately
   - Example: "Here's what nobody gonna tell you on TikTok" (curiosity) vs. "I'm going to teach you about AI" (boring, no curiosity)

3. **SIGNALS WHO THIS IS FOR** — So right person stays, wrong person scrolls
   - How: Direct callout in the first sentence
   - Not how: Generic language that could apply to anyone
   - Example: "If you work 9-5 and use ChatGPT..." (specific) vs. "Everyone needs to know this..." (generic)

## SECTION 2: THE 5-COMPONENT HOOK BREAKDOWN

Think of a hook like a machine with 5 parts. If ONE part fails, the whole machine breaks.

### COMPONENT 1: ON-SCREEN TEXT (The Visual Announcement)

This is literally the first thing people see. It's your headline. It's your first impression. You get 0.5 seconds.

#### The 5-Word Rule (Non-Negotiable)

**Count the words. Count them again. If it's 6, rewrite it.**

Why exactly 5 words?
- 1-2 words: Too vague, no context
- 3-4 words: Okay, but weak
- 5 words: Perfect. Memorable, specific, readable in under 0.5 seconds
- 6+ words: Too much cognitive load. Brain is still processing word 4 when word 6 appears. They scroll.

**Examples of GOOD 5-word text:**
- "Here's what nobody gonna tell you" (5 words) ✓
- "I got caught using AI today" (5 words) ✓
- "My boss still doesn't know this" (5 words) ✓
- "This is why your ex won" (5 words) ✓

**Examples of BAD text (too long):**
- "Here's what absolutely nobody on TikTok is telling you right now" (12 words) ✗
- "I got caught using ChatGPT at work and here's what happened" (11 words) ✗

See the difference? The long version makes you work to read it. The short version punches you.

#### Font Rules (Why It Matters)

You have ONE job: Maximum readability in minimum time.

**GOOD fonts:**
- Arial
- Helvetica
- System sans-serif (whatever your phone uses by default)
- Anything plain, bold, no-nonsense

**BAD fonts (NEVER use):**
- Impact (looks like a meme from 2010)
- Comic Sans (looks like a joke)
- Calligraphy (unreadable, feels fake)
- Anything "stylized" (your brain has to decode it; they scroll while decoding)

**Why?** Fancy fonts = cognitive load. Cognitive load = brain switches to processing the font instead of reading the words. While the brain is doing that, the thumb scrolls.

Plain fonts are boring. Boring is invisible. Invisible means readable. Readable means they stay.

#### High Contrast is Non-Negotiable

**What works:**
- White text on BLACK bubble (maximum contrast)
- Black text on WHITE bubble (maximum contrast)
- No colors except white and black

**What doesn't work:**
- Blue text on green background (low contrast, hard to read)
- White text on light gray (barely visible)
- Any gradient or semi-transparent background (adds complexity)

#### Animation: NONE

Here's the thing: Your brain processes movement differently than static text.

When the text ANIMATES in (fades, slides, types out word-by-word):
- Your brain is tracking the animation
- Your eyes follow the motion
- You're not reading the text, you're watching the animation happen
- While you're watching, you might scroll

When the text is STATIC (just appears, stays still):
- Your brain reads it instantly
- No distraction
- You stay

**Rule:** Make the text appear on frame at 0:00. It stays for the entire hook. No animation. No fade. No entry effect.

#### The Outlier Score: How to Know If Your Text Is Viral

Go to **oneoften.com**. Type your on-screen text. It gives you a score from -100 to +100.

**What this score means:**
- **-100 to -50:** This text is overused, boring, everyone says it. TikTok algorithm ignores it.
- **-50 to 0:** Okay, but not novel. Mid performance.
- **0 to +50:** Good. This is unique enough. Algorithm will test it.
- **+50 to +100:** VIRAL. This text is novel, unusual, unexpected. Algorithm will push it.

**Examples:**
- "You won't believe this" = -85 (overused, kills reach)
- "Nobody at my job knows I use this" = +72 (specific, unexpected, algo pushes)
- "Here's what nobody gonna tell you" = +68 (fresh phrasing, viral)
- "I got caught using AI and I'm glad" = +81 (vulnerable, specific, novel)

**Your job as editor:** Before you put text on screen, run it through oneoften. If it scores under 0, tell Tyler to rewrite it. Don't waste footage on low-scoring text.

### COMPONENT 2: VISUAL HOOK (What They're Watching While Reading)

While the text is on screen, what's happening in the background? This is the VISUAL part of the hook.

#### Background Selection (Why It Matters More Than You Think)

Studies show these backgrounds convert best:
1. **Office setting with warm light** (yellow, orange hue) — Signals "real person, real work, relatable"
2. **Car interior** (especially if it looks like a nice car) — Signals "real person, moving through life"
3. **Bedroom (but organized)** — Only works if it looks intentional, not lazy
4. **Outside, natural light** — Authentic, trustworthy

Backgrounds that DON'T work:
- Pristine studio or white room — Feels like an ad
- Dark, shadowy rooms — Untrustworthy
- Messy, cluttered background — Distraction, low production value
- Green screen by itself — Feels corporate, not real

**Why background matters:** Your subconscious brain makes a decision instantly. "Is this person real or selling me something?" If the background looks too perfect, your brain says "ad." If it looks real, your brain says "real person."

**In the edit:** If Tyler filmed in a boring background, you can't fix it. Tell him for next shoot: Use office with warm lamp. Use car. Use intentional space.

#### Movement in the Hook (First 2 Seconds)

Your body needs to MOVE in the first 2 seconds. Not after. Not on second 3. BEFORE second 3.

**Why?** Movement stops the scroll. Humans are wired to notice movement. It's survival instinct. A static shot of Tyler talking = easy scroll past. Tyler clapping = stops the thumb.

**Movements that work:**
- **Clapping** (sudden, sharp, gets attention)
- **Putting glasses on/off** (clear movement, takes 1 second)
- **Shaking/holding up something** (product, object, something tactile)
- **Pointing at camera** (creates direct connection)
- **Dipping fruit into something** (unexpected movement, creates curiosity)
- **Head movement** (turning to look at something, looking shocked)
- **Hand gestures** (not slow, deliberate; fast, natural)

**Movements that DON'T work:**
- **No movement** (static talking head = boring)
- **Slow-motion movement** (people are scrolling fast; slow-mo is too slow to catch them)
- **Looking away from camera** (kills connection, easy scroll)
- **Fake/staged movement** (people sense inauthenticity; they scroll)

#### High Motion + High Contrast

This is the principle: Your eye is drawn to change.

**High motion:** Something is happening. Not fast-motion, but real-time movement.
**High contrast:** The thing moving stands out from the background.

Example of HIGH motion + HIGH contrast:
- Tyler (wearing bright color) claps hands against neutral background
- Your eye is drawn to the clap (motion) and the bright color (contrast)
- Result: Scroll is stopped

Example of LOW motion + LOW contrast:
- Tyler (wearing neutral color) talks to camera in neutral room
- No motion (just talking), no contrast (everything blends together)
- Result: Easy scroll past

In your edit: If Tyler is wearing dark clothes in a dark background, make the shot brighter. Increase saturation slightly. Make him POP from the background. When he moves, make sure the movement is CLEAR.

#### The Rage Bait Principle (Advanced)

This is controversial but true: Hooks that trigger mild frustration or controversy get MORE engagement.

Example: "Expensive coffee shops are scamming you" = more comments than "I found a great coffee shop"

Why? Because people comment on things that trigger emotion. And comments = engagement. And engagement = algorithm push.

**But here's the line:** You're not trying to be inflammatory. You're trying to take a mildly contrarian position that makes people think "Oh, I never thought of it that way."

In the edit: If Tyler is saying something slightly against the grain (even if just in delivery), emphasize it. A slight smirk, a pause, a head shake. Make the mild controversy CLEAR so people feel it and react.

### COMPONENT 3: VERBAL HOOK (What Tyler Says in First 2 Seconds)

His words must accomplish three things:

1. **Direct callout to WHO this is for**
2. **A statement that's relatable but bold**
3. **Active voice (action, not description)**

#### Direct Callout Examples

These work because they FILTER the audience instantly.

- "If you work 9-5 and use ChatGPT..." — Now, non-9-5 workers scroll. 9-5 workers lean in.
- "Guys who got rejected know this..." — Women scroll. Men lean in. (Or reverse, if aimed at women.)
- "If you've ever tried to quit nicotine..." — Doesn't apply? Scroll. Applies? You're locked in.

**Why this works:** It's a filter. You're saying "This is for YOU specifically." Everyone else feels like it's not their problem. But the person it IS for feels SEEN. And when someone feels seen, they watch.

#### Bold But Relatable Language

You need vulnerability + confidence.

**Good examples:**
- "I'm not trying to scare you, but..." (vulnerability + warning)
- "Everyone's lying about this..." (confidence + revelation)
- "I did this for a week and here's what happened..." (vulnerability + curiosity)
- "I spent 3 years trying everything and then I found this..." (struggle + discovery)

**Bad examples:**
- "You won't believe this..." (overused, no specificity)
- "This is insane..." (vague, what is "it"?)
- "Let me explain..." (passive, weak)
- "This will change your life..." (claim-based, not experience-based)

The difference: Good examples are SPECIFIC and VULNERABLE. Bad examples are VAGUE and DETACHED.

#### Active Voice (Fewest Words)

**Active voice rule:** Subject does the action. Not passive.

- Active: "I discovered this by accident" (I = subject, discovered = action)
- Passive: "This was discovered by me" (This = subject, was discovered = action done TO it)

Active is faster, punchier, more direct.

**Word count rule:** Every word counts. If a word doesn't PUSH the idea forward, DELETE it.

- "I found a way to hide my AI use" = 8 words, all essential ✓
- "So there's actually this thing that's really kind of amazing and I want to tell you about it" = 17 words, 7 are filler ✗

In your edit: If Tyler has fluff words ("like," "um," "actually," "so basically," "kind of"), cut them. Sharp, direct language wins.

### COMPONENT 4: SUPER HOOK (The Curiosity Gap)

After the main hook, you need ONE more sentence that deepens the curiosity instead of answering it.

#### How Curiosity Gaps Work

Your job is to CREATE a question in their mind, then make them want to keep watching to ANSWER it.

**Bad structure:**
- Main hook: "I found a way to hide my AI use"
- Super hook: "I use Phrasly and it converts my text to human-sounding writing" (answered the question)
- Result: They know the answer. No reason to watch.

**Good structure:**
- Main hook: "I found a way to hide my AI use"
- Super hook: "And my boss still has no idea" (deepens the curiosity)
- Result: They wonder HOW, so they watch.

See the difference? Good super hooks DON'T answer. They deepen.

#### Super Hook Examples

- "And it only took me 3 weeks to figure it out" — Makes them want to know: How? What else did you learn?
- "My ex saw the results and asked me for advice" — Makes them want to know: What results? How did you change?
- "The algorithm absolutely hates this one trick" — Makes them want to know: What trick? Why does it hate it?

#### How to Write Them

Ask yourself: "What question does my main hook create?"

Main hook: "I quit my job" → Creates question: "Why?"
Super hook: "Here's what I didn't tell my boss" → Deepens question: "What didn't you tell them? What happened?"

Main hook: "I spent 3 years trying everything" → Creates question: "For what?"
Super hook: "Then I found this one product" → Deepens question: "What product? Does it actually work?"

The super hook should make them MORE curious, not less.

### COMPONENT 5: FORMAT (How to Deliver It)

There are 5 ways to film content. They convert in this order:

1. **Talking Head** (80% of viral content)
2. **Skit** (highest converting for conversions, but harder to film)
3. **Green Screen** (adds a layer; feels less real)
4. **Faceless** (no personal connection; lower conversion)
5. **Text-Only** (requires reading; lowest conversion)

#### Why Talking Head Wins

Talking head = You on camera, looking at camera, speaking directly to audience.

Why it converts best:
- **Direct connection** — You're looking at them. They feel seen.
- **Trust** — Seeing your face builds trust. Faceless doesn't.
- **Real** — Feels like a real person, not an ad.
- **Simple** — No production value needed. Just you, natural light, authenticity.

**Talking head failure points:**
- Looking away from camera (breaks connection)
- Fake energy (reads as salesy)
- Jerky movements or fidgeting (distracting)
- Bad lighting (looks cheap)

#### The Format Stacking Strategy

Once you find a WINNING script, you remake it in other formats.

Example:
- Tyler films "How I use AI at work" as talking head
- It gets 10k views (winner)
- Now you film the SAME SCRIPT as:
  - Green screen version
  - Voiceover + B-roll version
  - Text-only version
  - Skit version

You're not creating new ideas. You're OPTIMIZING the winning idea across formats.

This is how you scale fast. Not by inventing. By remixing.

## SECTION 3: THE 70% PROBLEM RULE (Why Most Videos Fail)

This is where your instincts are WRONG. You need to rewire your brain.

### The Core Principle

**70% of your video should be about the PROBLEM. Only 30% is solution/transformation.**

This feels backwards. Your instinct is: "Show the product! Make it look good! Speed through the boring problems."

WRONG. The product is boring. The PROBLEM is interesting.

### What "Problem" Means

Problem footage is:
- Tyler talking about frustration (his tone, his words)
- Tyler explaining what he TRIED that DIDN'T work
- Tyler describing the emotional weight of the problem
- Objections (reasons why it wouldn't work)
- Examples of alternatives that failed

Problem footage is NOT:
- Product on screen
- Tyler using/holding the product
- Before/after
- "This solved it"

### How to Recognize Problem vs. Solution in Raw Footage

You'll get raw footage from Tyler. Some clips are problem. Some are solution.

**Problem clip examples:**
- "I tried everything for 3 years. Nothing worked." (problem)
- "My coworkers kept catching me using ChatGPT" (problem)
- "I was so paranoid they'd find out" (problem, emotional)
- "I downloaded 5 different tools and they all sucked" (problem, alternatives failed)

**Solution clip examples:**
- "Then I found Phrasly" (solution intro)
- "I put my text in and it came out human" (solution, product in action)
- "Now I'm not paranoid anymore" (transformation)
- "It literally changed how I work" (transformation claim)

### The 70/30 Split In Practice

Let's say Tyler gives you a 60-second raw recording.

**Wrong edit (what your instinct says):**
- 0-10 seconds: Hook (problem)
- 10-25 seconds: Product intro + demo (solution)
- 25-45 seconds: Benefits + transformation (solution)
- 45-60 seconds: CTA (solution)

Result: Only 10 seconds problem, 50 seconds solution. Video FLOPS.

**Right edit (70/30 rule):**
- 0-2 seconds: Hook (problem)
- 2-45 seconds: Stacking problem statements, objections, alternatives that failed (problem = 43 seconds)
- 45-52 seconds: Product reveal + brief explanation (solution = 7 seconds)
- 52-60 seconds: Transformation + CTA (solution = 8 seconds)

Result: 43 seconds problem, 15 seconds solution. Video CONVERTS.

### Why This Works (The Psychology)

Your brain works like this:

1. **If I don't feel the problem, I don't need the solution**
2. **If I FEEL the problem deeply, I'm desperate for the solution**
3. **The longer I sit with the problem, the more I need the solution**

By spending 70% on problem, you're doing emotional work. You're making them FEEL the frustration, the failed alternatives, the hopelessness.

Then, when the solution appears, it's not an ad. It's a RELIEF. It's the answer to the question they're now asking.

That's conversion psychology.

### Editing Rule for Problem Footage

If Tyler's face shows frustration while talking about the problem, DO NOT CUT IT.

Let it breathe. Let people sit with the frustration. The discomfort is the point. The discomfort is what makes them need the solution.

New editors always want to "move it along" and "keep the energy up." Wrong. Let the problem HURT a little.

## SECTION 4: PACING & RETENTION

This is where audio editing, cuts, and timing come together.

### The 50-75 Second Target

Aim for 50-75 seconds total. Not 90. Not 60-90.

Why?
- Under 50 seconds: Feels rushed, not enough time to build tension
- 50-75 seconds: Sweet spot. Long enough to tell a story, short enough to hold attention
- 75-90 seconds: You're testing attention limits. Some people scroll.
- Over 90 seconds: You've lost most people.

TikTok ALLOWS 90 seconds. That doesn't mean use it. Use the time you NEED, not the time you have.

### The Cut Rule: Delete Everything That Doesn't Move the Story

This is the hardest part.

Go through the raw footage. Every "um," every breath, every pause where Tyler is thinking — CUT IT.

**Footage that stays:**
- Tyler speaking problem/objection/solution (speaking footage)
- Tyler's face showing emotion that reinforces the problem (reaction footage)
- B-roll that demonstrates something he's saying (if needed)

**Footage that gets cut:**
- "Um," "uh," "like," "actually," "so basically" (filler words)
- Pauses longer than 0.5 seconds (dead air)
- Tyler looking confused or thinking (unless it reinforces the problem)
- Any moment that doesn't move the story forward

**The test:** If the video makes sense without this clip, CUT IT.

### Transitions: Hard Cuts Only

You have three transition options:

1. **Hard cut** (cut immediately to next clip) ✓ USE THIS
2. **Fade to black** (fade to black for 0.2 seconds, then fade in) ✓ USE THIS SPARINGLY
3. **Anything else** (zoom, spin, slide, etc.) ✗ NEVER USE

Why hard cuts?
- Immediate, no processing time
- Feels energetic and real
- Keeps pace tight
- No distraction

Why fade-to-black sometimes?
- Can work as a pause/reset
- Signals a topic change
- But overuse it and it slows momentum

Why nothing else?
- Zoom/spin/slide = old, cringe
- Forces brain to process an animation
- Takes processing power away from content
- Feels cheap and overproduced

**Rule:** 95% hard cuts. 5% fade-to-black. Everything else is deleted.

### Music & Audio Levels

**Rule: Voice always wins. Music is support.**

If Tyler is talking, music should be barely audible. Like, you have to listen closely to notice it's there.

**Volume rule:**
- Tyler's voice: 100% (full volume)
- Music: 2-5% (barely audible)
- Sound effects: 0% (don't use unless Tyler approved it)

**How to set this:**
- Import Tyler's audio
- Import music track
- Lower music volume to -20dB (that's roughly 5% volume)
- Test: Listen. If music is obvious, lower it more.

**Music selection:**
- Lo-fi beats (chill, not energetic)
- No trending sounds (they date fast)
- No energetic music (makes it feel like an ad)
- Silent is often BETTER (just his voice)

**Why silent can be better:** Silence creates tension. Silence makes you lean in. Music can distract from the message.

In fact: If Tyler's voice is compelling, the video doesn't need music. Try editing without it first.

### Captions

Captions serve two purposes:
1. Accessibility (people watch with sound off)
2. Emphasis (key moments stick)

**Caption rules:**
- Clean up auto-captions (they're usually 70% right, 30% wrong)
- 4-5 words per line, maximum
- One line at a time (stagger them, don't overlap)
- White text, bold, sans-serif, bottom third of screen
- Sync to speech (caption appears when Tyler says the word)

**Emphasis rule:** If Tyler emphasizes a word, bold JUST that word.

Example:
- Tyler says: "This is REALLY important"
- Caption: "This is **really** important"

**What NOT to do:**
- Emojis (it's not 2019)
- Multiple lines overlapping (hard to read)
- Fancy fonts (unreadable)
- Animations (distraction)
- Captions covering Tyler's face (move them lower)

## SECTION 5: THE CALLAWAY 7 LEGO BRICKS (How to Study Winners)

When Tyler has a winning video, you need to study it and understand WHY it won. Then, you remake it.

The Callaway 7 Bricks are the framework for breaking down what makes it work.

### The 7 Bricks

1. **Topic** — What is the video ABOUT?
   - "How to use AI at work without getting caught"
   - "Why Phrasly converts your AI-written text"
   - "How I hide my chatGPT usage"

2. **Angle** — What's the UNIQUE PERSPECTIVE?
   - "From a corporate worker's POV" (not "from a tech expert's POV")
   - "As someone paranoid about being discovered" (not "as a power user")
   - "From someone who's tried 5 tools before this" (not "from a first-time user")
   
   The angle is WHO is telling the story, not just what the story is.

3. **Hook** — What's the FIRST SENTENCE/TEXT that stops the scroll?
   - "Nobody at my job knows I use this"
   - "I got caught using AI and I'm glad"
   - "Here's what nobody gonna tell you"

4. **Story Structure** — How does the narrative PROGRESS?
   - Setup problem (why I'm frustrated)
   - Stack objections (I tried this and it didn't work, I tried that and it didn't work)
   - Show solution (then I found this)
   - Show result (now I feel this way)
   
   This is the SHAPE of the narrative, not the details.

5. **Visual Format** — TALKING HEAD? SKIT? GREEN SCREEN?
   - Just a key: Which format was used?

6. **Key Visuals** — What B-roll, cuts, or moments DRIVE IT?
   - Close-up of product?
   - Screen recording of software?
   - Tyler's face reacting?
   - Quick cuts or longer holds?

7. **Audio** — What music or sound design?
   - Silent (just voice)?
   - Lo-fi background (barely audible)?
   - Specific sound effects?
   - Trending audio?

### How to Study a Winner (Step by Step)

1. **Watch it 3 times without pausing**
   - First watch: Feel the vibe
   - Second watch: Notice where you lean in
   - Third watch: Start to notice patterns

2. **On fourth watch, pause every 5 seconds. Write down what's happening in each brick**
   - At 0-5s: Hook text is "Nobody at my job knows I use this" (on-screen text). Format is talking head. Background is office. Movement is Tyler looking at camera.
   - At 5-10s: Audio starts (lo-fi, barely audible). Story is starting problem: "I was paranoid about getting caught."
   - Etc.

3. **After full breakdown, ask: Which bricks are STRONG? Which are WEAK?**
   - Strong: Hook is tight. Story structure is clear. Pacing is good.
   - Weak: Audio is forgettable. Background is boring. Transitions feel slow.

4. **Remake the STRONG bricks EXACTLY. Improve the WEAK ones.**
   - Copy the strong stuff. Don't reinvent.
   - Fix the weak stuff. That's where you add your own edge.

### Example: How to Remix a Winner

Let's say Tyler's Phrasly video goes viral.

**You break it down into 7 bricks:**
- Topic: "How to use AI at work without getting caught"
- Angle: "Corporate worker paranoid about discovery"
- Hook: "Nobody at my job knows I use this"
- Story: Setup paranoia → Tried ChatGPT, got caught → Tried other tools, still paranoid → Phrasly → Feels confident
- Format: Talking head in office
- Visuals: 2-sec cuts of Tyler at desk, 3-sec close-up of Phrasly converting text, 2-sec reaction shot
- Audio: Lo-fi, 3% volume

**Now you make the SAME STRUCTURE with a DIFFERENT PRODUCT:**
- Topic: "How I use [Different AI Tool] without getting caught"
- Angle: "Same — corporate worker paranoid"
- Hook: Different text, but same structure (5 words, outlier score 60+)
- Story: SAME structure (paranoia → failed alternatives → solution → relief)
- Format: SAME — talking head in office
- Visuals: SIMILAR timings and cuts
- Audio: SIMILAR — lo-fi, minimal

You're not copying. You're using the STRUCTURE of what works, then filling in new content.

This is how you scale fast.

## SECTION 6: THE CALLAWAY HOOK FORMULA (3 Aligned Components)

A viral hook has THREE components that MUST ALIGN.

If they DON'T align, the hook fails, and the video dies.

### The 3 Components

1. **VISUAL** — High motion + High contrast
2. **TEXT** — Context + Curiosity gap
3. **SPOKEN** — Active voice + Fewest words

### Component 1: Visual (High Motion + High Contrast)

**Motion:** Movement in frame (not slow-mo, real-time movement)
**Contrast:** The thing moving stands out from background (color, light, action)

Example of ALIGNED visual:
- Tyler (wearing bright color) claps hands (motion) against neutral background (contrast)
- Result: Your eye is PULLED to the motion and the contrast

Example of MISALIGNED visual:
- Tyler (wearing neutral color) sits still (no motion) in neutral room (no contrast)
- Result: Your eye has no reason to stay. Easy scroll.

In editing: Make sure the person MOVING stands out from the BACKGROUND. If Tyler's wearing dark clothes in a dark background, brighten the shot or increase saturation so he POPS.

### Component 2: Text (Context + Curiosity Gap)

**Context:** Who is this for? (e.g., "If you work in tech...")
**Curiosity gap:** What don't I know? (e.g., "...here's why you're underpaid")

Example of ALIGNED text:
- "If you work 9-5 and use ChatGPT" (context — narrow audience)
- "Here's what your boss doesn't want you to know" (curiosity — question created)
- Result: Right person feels called out. They lean in.

Example of MISALIGNED text:
- "You won't believe this" (no context — could be anyone)
- "I found something cool" (no curiosity — vague)
- Result: Generic. No reason to stay.

In editing: The on-screen text MUST have context + curiosity. If it doesn't, ask Tyler to reshoot.

### Component 3: Spoken (Active Voice + Fewest Words)

**Active voice:** Subject does the action (not passive descriptions)
**Fewest words:** Every word counts. Delete filler.

Example of ALIGNED spoken:
- "I discovered this by accident" (active, 5 words, all essential)
- Result: Direct, punchy, clear

Example of MISALIGNED spoken:
- "So there's actually this thing that's kind of really cool and I want to tell you about it" (passive, 17 words, 7 are filler)
- Result: Slow, vague, people scroll while you're talking

In editing: Cut filler words ("um," "like," "actually," "basically," "so"). Sharp, direct language wins.

### When All 3 Align

Visual + Text + Spoken all working together = VIRAL HOOK

The formula: Direct contrast > implied. +100 to -100 = 200 arc (full spectrum of emotion in the hook alone).

Translation: The hook should swing from intense problem (+100) to intense curiosity/hope (-100), or vice versa. That swing is what catches people.

## SECTION 7: THE VOLUME METHOD (Why You Need 500 Reps)

This is the hardest truth: You will not be elite in 2 weeks.

## SECTION 8: COMMON MISTAKES (The Kill List)

These things will MURDER your video. Watch for them.

### 1. Em Dashes (-)
Never use em dashes. Anywhere. In captions, in notes, nowhere.

Why? They don't appear correctly on TikTok captions. They look broken. They're a distraction.

Use periods or line breaks instead.

### 2. Emojis in Captions
It's 2026. Emojis in captions feel dated and cheap.

Use text only. Let the words stand alone.

### 3. Fancy Fonts
Impact. Comic Sans. Calligraphy. Anything "stylized."

Your brain has to decode fancy fonts. While decoding, people scroll.

Use plain sans-serif only (Arial, Helvetica, system fonts).

### 4. Vignettes or Blur Effects
Vignettes (darkened edges) are distracting and old.

Blur effects (blurring background) are distracting and feel like a filter.

Keep video sharp and clear.

### 5. Filters on Skin
Beauty filters, skin smoothing, etc.

These make people look LESS like themselves. They feel fake. They kill trust.

Film Tyler as he is. Natural light. No filters.

### 6. Watermarks
TikTok users won't share videos with watermarks.

Don't watermark your edits.

### 7. Music Louder Than 10%
If music is audible, it's competing with Tyler's voice.

Voice always wins. Music is support.

Keep music at 2-5% volume.

### 8. Transitions Other Than Hard Cuts or Fade-to-Black
Zoom. Spin. Slide. Wipe.

These are old, cringe, and distracting.

Use hard cuts 95% of the time. Fade-to-black 5%.

### 9. Logos or Title Cards
"Tyler's Channel" title card at the start.

Unnecessary. Slows momentum. People scroll while it's on screen.

Jump straight into the hook.

### 10. Trying to Save a Bad Hook in the Edit
If the hook is weak, you can't fix it in post.

Tell Tyler it's weak. Ask him to reshoot. Don't waste time trying to salvage it.

## SECTION 9: THE EDITING CHECKLIST (Before Every Export)

Before you export ANY video, go through this checklist. If you can't check all boxes, it's not done.

### The Checklist

- [ ] **Does the hook stop the scroll in 2 seconds?**
  - Is there movement in the first 2 seconds?
  - Is on-screen text high contrast and 5 words or less?
  - Does the text create curiosity?
  - If no to any, it fails. Don't export.

- [ ] **Are the first 70% about the PROBLEM? (Not the product.)**
  - How much time is spent on problem vs. solution?
  - If solution starts before the 70% mark, restructure.

- [ ] **Is the product reveal EARNED?**
  - Did the problem section build enough tension that the solution feels like relief?
  - Or does the product feel random?

- [ ] **Is on-screen text 5 words max?**
  - Count again. If it's 6, rewrite.

- [ ] **Is pacing tight? (No dead air, no unnecessary pauses.)**
  - Watch the video at normal speed. Does it feel like it's dragging?
  - Are there any "um"s or long pauses? Cut them.

- [ ] **Is audio at right level?**
  - Can you hear Tyler's voice clearly?
  - Is music barely audible (2-5% volume)?

- [ ] **Are transitions only hard cuts or fade-to-black?**
  - Any zoom/spin/slide? Delete them.

- [ ] **Does the ending hard-cut on the CTA?**
  - Is there an outro after the CTA? Delete it.
  - Does the video end abruptly on "click the link" or "buy now"?

- [ ] **Does it feel like a real person, not an ad?**
  - Watch it once as a stranger (don't think about technique).
  - Does it feel authentic or salesy?

If you can't check all boxes, send it back to Tyler with specific notes. Don't export until it's done.
